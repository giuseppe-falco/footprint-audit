# Removal templates (EU)

Check that every link still works before proposing it: forms change. Mark any unverified URL as "to verify".

## Erasure request under GDPR art. 17

Subject: Request for erasure of personal data under Article 17 GDPR

Dear <site owner / data controller>,

I, <Full Name>, request under Article 17 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) the erasure of the personal data concerning me published at the following addresses:
- <URL 1>: <data, e.g. name, phone number, photo>
- <URL 2>: <…>

Under Article 19, I also ask you to notify the erasure to any recipients to whom the data have been disclosed. Please confirm the erasure within one month of receipt of this request (Article 12(3)).

For identification: <only the minimum needed, e.g. the email address linked to the account; never an ID document unless strictly necessary>.

If I do not receive a reply within this period, I reserve the right to lodge a complaint with the competent supervisory authority.

Kind regards,
<Full Name>

Notes:
- Send the request to the privacy or DPO address given in the site's privacy policy, and keep the date it was sent.
- Share only data that are already exposed. Some dubious data brokers use removal requests to collect more data.
- Write in the site's language when possible. Country modules may provide a translated version.

## Complaint to the supervisory authority
Lodge a complaint if the controller does not answer within one month (extendable by two further months in complex cases) or refuses. Attach the request you sent and evidence of the publication (dated screenshots). The national authorities are listed on edpb.europa.eu.

## Search engines
- **Google "Results about you":** myactivity.google.com/results-about-you. It covers results showing your phone number, email address or home address, and includes monitoring.
- **Google, right to be forgotten (EU):** the "Personal information removal request" form in the Google Help Center. It applies to content that is no longer relevant, even when it is true.
- **Google, pages already removed from the site but still in results or cache:** the outdated content tool at search.google.com/search-console/remove-outdated-content.
- **Bing:** the Microsoft "Report a Concern" or Content Removal form, EU right-to-be-forgotten version.

## archive.org (Wayback Machine)
Write to info@archive.org as the owner of the content or the data subject. List the exact URLs (for example `https://web.archive.org/web/*/example.com/<handle>*`) and ask for removal or exclusion.

## GitHub
- Settings to change by hand: Settings → Emails → "Keep my email addresses private" and, if needed, "Block command line pushes that expose my email".
- Profile: decide whether to keep location and company.
- To purge old SHAs after a force push, or cached sensitive commits, contact GitHub Support (support.github.com, "Remove sensitive data").
