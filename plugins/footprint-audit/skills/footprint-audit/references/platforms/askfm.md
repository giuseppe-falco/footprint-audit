# ask.fm

Last verified: 2026-11-11. Links and menu paths change: re-check before proposing them.

## Status of the service
- The original ASKfm (operated from Dublin, Ireland, owned by IAC since 2014 per Wikipedia) announced closure on 2024-10-22; registrations stopped 2024-10-24 and the platform shut down on 2024-12-01. Users were told to export content beforehand and that personal data would be deleted "as soon as reasonably practicable", some types up to 90 days.
- A service branded ASKfm now runs at `https://askfm.app` (footer "© 2026 ASKfm LLC"; no address or jurisdiction shown on the home page). Wikipedia reports it as a 2026 revival by a different group; this is a single secondary source. Whether it is connected to the old database or old profiles is unconfirmed (to verify). Do not assume old `ask.fm/<username>` profiles were migrated, and do not register to find out.

## See yourself as a stranger
- Search `site:ask.fm "<username>"` and the name in a search engine; old `ask.fm/<username>` pages may only survive in search caches and on web.archive.org.
- Check `https://askfm.app` public pages for the handle (read-only, no login).
- Old answers, photos and questions were public and often mirrored by scrapers and archives (ArchiveTeam has a page on ASK.fm).

## What to check (logged in, in the user's own session)
- Only applicable if the user holds an account on askfm.app (to verify whether old accounts exist).
- Bio, avatar, linked social accounts (Facebook, Instagram, VK, Twitter/X), answers and photos, anonymous question settings.
- Third-party logins that may still be connected: revoke ask.fm in those accounts' "apps and websites" settings.

## Settings to tighten
- Privacy controls: the policy says that, "depending on the available features", users may disable anonymous questions, block users, manage notifications and delete questions and answers; no search-engine opt-out is mentioned, and public content "may also appear in search engine results". Exact menu labels need a logged-in account (to verify).
- Unlink social accounts.

## Get your data (export / GDPR art. 15)
- Old operator: Ask.fm Europe Limited (Dublin). Privacy contact in the legacy policy: `support@ask.fm` (the legacy site is no longer reachable; not re-checked, to verify).
- New operator: ASKfm LLC (U.S.). Privacy policy `https://askfm.app/privacy-policy` and contact `https://askfm.app/contact`. Read in browser on 2026-11-11 (effective 2026-10-19): the policy names only "ASKfm" with no legal entity, address, EU representative or DPO, and no controller statement; the Terms name no entity either. Section 11 "Your EU Data Protection Rights" lists access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and says to "contact us". The only channel is the web form at `https://askfm.app/contact` (e-mail + subject + message; subjects include "Bullying", "Report a violation", "Report a fake account"; no privacy/GDPR subject). Send an art. 15/17 request through it with a clear subject line and keep the dated copy.

## Remove
- Account deletion on askfm.app: the policy (section 10) says users "may deactivate their accounts through their account settings" where supported and "may request the deletion of their accounts and associated personal information"; requests are processed "in accordance with applicable laws and our data retention requirements"; some data may remain in backups or security records and public content may persist in third-party copies or search caches. No grace period or menu path is stated. Legacy steps were Settings > deactivate/leave, final after 30 days, backups up to 90 days.
- Content already gone or account already deleted: copies may remain on search engines and archive.org. Use `references/removal-eu.md` for: search-engine delisting (Google "Results about you", EU form, outdated content tool, Bing), archive.org removal request by the content owner, and an art. 17 erasure request to any scraper/mirror site.
- Unlink social profiles and revoke third-party app access in the other platforms.
